## Supplemental primary sources consulted

1. Academy of Motion Picture Arts and Sciences, **Screenplay format guide / sample script** (Greg Beal / April Rider), https://www.oscars.org/sites/oscars/files/scriptsample.pdf . Read for screenplay headings, action, speaker cues, readable page conventions, and the guide's explicit recognition of acceptable variation. The app gives an original concise explanation, not a reproduction of this script.
2. **Fountain official syntax**, https://fountain.io/syntax/ . Read for plain-text screenplay components and export conventions: title metadata, scene headings, blank-line-separated action, uppercase character cues followed by dialogue, and optional parentheticals. The studio exports basic Fountain; it does not claim full parser support in its preview.

## Instructional choices and corrections
- The handbook's dramatic model is taught as a useful starting method, not a universal definition excluding experimental films, anecdotes, or portraits.
- Character change need not be a compulsory moral conversion. A changed relationship, situation, or audience understanding can matter.
- A protagonist's flaw is not compulsory; a stable value under pressure can be dramatic.
- One page per minute is approximate. Dense action may take a long time, and dialogue delivery varies. The app requires timing observations rather than a false exact runtime estimate.
- The mute test, cast/location limits, 4–8 scene suggestion, opening attention window, and 30% cut target are exercises or heuristics, not laws.
- Dialogue, direct emotional speech, voiceover, music, and quiet atmosphere can be valid intentional choices. Exercises develop alternatives without banning those techniques.
- The user wants ability transfer. Quiz recognition is followed by original writing, snapshots, read-through observations, revision notes, a pitch, and a ten-part evidence review.
- Completion is not an automated claim of professional readiness, marketability, artistic quality, or festival acceptance.

## Original worked example
The Spare Key is a new teaching script with a logline, spine, treatment, scenes, and commentary. The commentary includes questions for further staging/revision so it does not imply that a demonstration is beyond criticism.
